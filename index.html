The Studio

Started Crooked.
Fired Anyway.

It started crooked. Literally.

The first piece ever made at Eight Feathers Studio was a simple slab — hand pressed, organically edged, imperfect by any production standard. Into it were carved three things: a declaration of love for Button, the year 2026, and the raven maker's stamp pressed into the lower left corner. Then it was set aside to dry, and attention turned to the kiln.

The kiln itself was a character — a rescued L&L Easy-Fire, worth $4,500 new, acquired with all its history intact. Chipped bricks. A lid that didn't sit quite flush. A keypad with a stuck button that needed a ribbon cleaning just to accept a firing program. A kiln with stories already in its walls before a single Eight Feathers piece ever touched its shelves.

The first firing attempt failed. Heat escaped through the lid gap faster than the elements could compensate. The kiln gave up before reaching temperature. The lid was sealed with ceramic fiber rope, a weight added to compress it, and the attempt began again.

The second firing started at 4:16 in the afternoon. Through the night the temperature climbed — slowly, steadily, stubbornly. At midnight it passed 1,000°F. By early morning it reached the point where the first firing had failed. This time it kept climbing.

At 7:25 AM on June 6th, 2026 — after 14 hours and 43 minutes — the display read CPLT.

When the kiln was opened and the Button piece brought outside, the warp was dramatic and obvious and perfect. The clay had moved during firing the way clay moves — on its own terms, following its own logic. The organic edge was more organic than ever. The raven stamp had survived. The declaration of love was still there, fired permanently into bisque.

Zappho and Cheese supervised from the garden fence, completely unbothered.

"Started Crooked. Fired Anyway."

How It's Made

From Earth to Wind

01
Shaping

Each bell is hand-built without molds. Pinch, coil, and slab construction — the form emerges from the clay itself. No two are identical.

02
Signing

Eight dots pressed in an arc. The raven stamp. The maker's mark circle. Three elements that appear on every piece before it dries.

03
Bisque Fire

First firing to Cone 06. The clay hardens to bisque — porous, ready for glaze. The ring test confirms the fire was true.

04
Glazing

Iron oxide wash wiped into every recess. Stoneware glazes layered by hand. Each bell receives its surface identity — dark, earthy, complex.

05
Cone 6 Fire

Final firing to 2232°F. The glaze melts and fuses permanently with the clay body. The bell becomes stone.

06
Assembly

Reclaimed copper hardware. Hand-formed S-hooks. Waxed cord. Each clapper chosen for the bell's specific tone and form. Assembled by hand, one bell at a time.

What Goes Into Each Bell

Materials & Provenance

🏺
Stoneware Clay

Cone 6 stoneware body. Dense, durable, permanent after firing. Each bell is vitrified — it will not absorb water or degrade outdoors.

🔩
Reclaimed Copper

Sourced from salvage yards. Copper tubing, sheet, fittings — all carrying real patina from real use. Nothing artificially aged.

⚗️
Iron Oxide

Red iron oxide wash applied and wiped back. Pools in carved recesses, lifts from raised surfaces. The oldest ceramic colorant in history.

🪵
Wine Oak Staves

Reclaimed from California wine barrels. Used as texture paddles, clapper fins, and wind sails. The oak grain transfers permanently into wet clay.

🧵
Waxed Linen Cord

Traditional bookbinding material. Strong, minimal stretch, weather resistant. Dark brown or black — nearly invisible against the dark clay body.

✦
Brass Hardware

Casings, ferrules, and decorative elements sourced from reclaimed brass. Mixed patina states — bright, aged, verdigris — tell the story of time.

The Voice of Each Bell

Tone & Sound

Watch — Bell in the Garden Breeze

Each bell is tuned using a chromatic analyzer before assembly. The note, frequency, and tonal character are documented and included with every piece.

B6
EF-001 — The Egg Bell
2029.7 Hz  ·  +10 cents

Bright, high, clean chime. Fast decay. The sound of a small, perfectly contained resonant chamber.

G#1
EF-002 — The Seedpod Bell
52.8 Hz  ·  −10 cents

Deep, warm, bass gong. Slow resonant decay. The rocking pillow clapper produces a soft interior strike — the sound seems to come from inside the earth.

A note on tuning

No two handmade bells ring the same note. The tone is a result of the specific form, wall thickness, clay body, and glaze combination — variables that exist in every unique piece. Each bell's tone is discovered, not designed.
